SECTION 1

What is this site?

Herbal Almanac is a read-only research library of traditional Ayurvedic plant (dravya) records scraped directly from the official CCRAS DRAVYA portal (Central Council for Research in Ayurvedic Sciences, Ministry of Ayush, Government of India).

The system systematically structures and connects medicinal plants, traditional illness and indication references, classical Dravyaguna pharmacology (Rasa, Guna, Virya, Vipaka, Prabhava, Karma), and directional Dosha effects (Vata, Pitta, Kapha pacification or aggravation).

Designed for students, researchers, and developers, Herbal Almanac combines traditional Ayurvedic terminology with rigorous software engineering: immutable claim-level provenance tracking, normalized semantic catalog ontologies, automated Virya multi-source reconciliation, and multi-script translations across 15 Indian languages.

SECTION 3

Glossary of Terms

19 Core Concepts

Descriptive definitions of the classical Ayurvedic and architectural terms used across this library:

AyurvedaTRADITIONAL SYSTEM

The classical Indian system of holistic healthcare, documented in foundational Sanskrit treatises (such as the Charaka Samhita, Sushruta Samhita, and Astanga Hridaya) over several millennia.

DravyaMATERIA MEDICA

A medicinal substance or material (principally botanical plants, parts, or minerals) possessing inherent pharmacological properties (Gunas) and therapeutic actions (Karmas).

DravyagunaPHARMACOLOGY

The classical Ayurvedic science of pharmacology and materia medica (Dravyaguna Vijnana), which studies the identification, properties, actions, and therapeutic applications of dravyas.

Rasa (Taste)GUSTATORY SENSATION

The immediate taste perceived by the tongue upon contact with a herb. Ayurveda identifies six primary tastes: Madhura (Sweet), Amla (Sour), Lavana (Salty), Katu (Pungent), Tikta (Bitter), and Kashaya (Astringent), each acting as a clinical indicator of pharmacological effects.

Guna (Qualities)PHYSICAL ATTRIBUTES

The intrinsic physical and bio-energetic qualities of a herb (e.g., Guru / Heavy vs. Laghu / Light, Snigdha / Unctuous vs. Ruksha / Dry), classically catalogued into 10 opposing pairs (20 total gunas) that influence bodily tissues.

Virya (Potency, Ushna/Sheeta)THERMAL POTENCY

The fundamental active thermodynamic and metabolic potency through which a herb performs its physiological action, primarily classified as Ushna (heating / hot) or Sheeta (cooling / cold).

Vipaka (Post-Digestive Effect)METABOLIC TASTE

The transformed taste and biochemical effect that manifests after a substance has undergone complete gastric digestion and assimilation (Jatharagni paka), classified into Madhura (sweet), Amla (sour), or Katu (pungent).

PrabhavaSPECIFIC ACTION

The unique, idiosyncratic, or specific therapeutic action of a herb that cannot be predicted or explained strictly by the combined sum of its Rasa, Guna, Virya, and Vipaka alone.

Dosha (Vata, Pitta, Kapha) and "Dosha Effects"BIOLOGICAL ENERGIES

The three regulatory psycho-physiological energies (Tridosha) governing bodily functions. "Dosha effects" record whether a herb pacifies/decreases (shamana, hara, ghna) or aggravates/increases (vardhaka, vrddhi) Vata (movement), Pitta (metabolism/heat), or Kapha (structure/fluid balance).

Karma / Therapeutic ActionPHYSIOLOGICAL ACTION

The clinical or physiological action produced by a herb, such as Deepana (appetite kindling), Pachana (digestive processing), or Rasayana (rejuvenating / adaptogenic nourishment).

Part Used / Plant PartOFFICINAL MORPHOLOGY

The specific botanical organ of the medicinal plant employed in formulations (Prayojyāṅga), such as Moola (root), Patra (leaf), Phala (fruit), Twak (bark), or Beeja (seed).

Preparation / KalpanaPHARMACEUTICAL FORM

The traditional pharmaceutical formulation process or dosage form applied to plant materials, such as Churna (fine powder), Kwatha (decoction), Swarasa (fresh juice), or Taila (medicated oil).

Indication / Therapeutic Usage / IllnessCLINICAL CONDITION

Pathological conditions, clinical symptoms, or disorders (Roga) for which classical Ayurvedic texts recommend the administration of a particular medicinal plant.

Vernacular Name / Sanskrit Name / Botanical Name / FamilyNOMENCLATURE

The multi-tiered naming taxonomy: standardized Latin botanical binomial and plant family (e.g. Ocimum tenuiflorum, Lamiaceae), classical Sanskrit names (in Devanagari and transliteration), and regional vernacular names across Indian states.

Concept (Canonical Concept) and ClaimKNOWLEDGE ARCHITECTURE

In our architecture, a Claim is an atomic assertion extracted verbatim from a primary source document (e.g., "potency is Ushna"), while a Concept is a standardized, language-agnostic ontology node (e.g. PROP_VIRYA_USHNA) to which claims are mapped.

Source / ProvenanceAUDIT TRAIL

The exact digital monograph, portal record, or classical text from which a botanical assertion was extracted, providing an immutable audit trail from source text to UI presentation.

Reconciliation (What "Virya Reconciliation" Shows)SOURCE CONFLICT AUDIT

An automated comparison report that evaluates multiple independent classical treatises or monograph claims for a herb, identifying unanimous concordance, candidate divergence under review, or verified historical source disagreements.

Unreviewed / Unverified / Machine-Generated LabelsEPISTEMIC STATUS

Status indicators explaining data certainty: Machine-generated signifies translations produced by automated AI models (such as Gemini or Google Translate); Unreviewed or Unverified signifies scraped records that have not yet been evaluated by certified domain scholars or clinical trials.

Original Term vs. Resolved English/Hindi NameTRANSLITERATION

The raw scraped transliteration (often featuring Harvard-Kyoto or ITRANS capital letters such as KAsa or jvaraH) preserved verbatim for scholarly integrity, alongside human-readable English names ("Cough", "Fever") and localized vernacular scripts resolved by our glossary engine.

SECTION 4

Data and Sources

The data presented in Herbal Almanac originates from three distinct layers:

  1. CCRAS DRAVYA Portal: Primary botanical records, morphology, and therapeutic attributes scraped from the Central Council for Research in Ayurvedic Sciences (Ministry of Ayush, Government of India).
  2. Curated Multilingual Concept Catalog: 136 canonical Stage C concepts spanning 11 pharmacological domains, with 649 scholarly bilingual/multilingual labels covering English (Latin), Hindi (Devanagari), and Sanskrit (Devanagari and IAST). Curated catalog labels take strict precedence on conflict.
  3. Multilingual Illness Glossary: A 227-condition translation glossary generated with AI assistance via Gemini Deep Research. These translations are machine-generated and not clinically verified.

Limits and Safety Boundaries

Important Research Disclaimer: Herbal Almanac is strictly an academic research and software engineering evaluation project. It is not a medical prescribing tool, diagnostic device, or clinical decision support system.
  • Unverified Associations: Therapeutic indications and herb-disease links are scraped from external web portals and may be incomplete, unverified, or subject to conflicting classical interpretations.
  • No Medical Advice: Nothing on this website constitutes medical advice, clinical diagnosis, prevention, or treatment recommendations.
  • No Recommendation Algorithms: The platform contains no symptom-matching or automated prescribing algorithms.
  • Consult Professionals: Always consult a qualified, registered Ayurvedic physician or healthcare professional before making any health or medicinal decisions.
